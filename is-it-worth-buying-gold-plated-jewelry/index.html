Is It Worth Buying Gold-Plated Jewelry?

Short answer: sometimes. Gold-plated jewelry is a smart buy for trends, earrings and occasional wear, and a poor one for rings you wear every day. Here is how to decide.

By McGee Jewelry · Updated October 2026

What gold plating actually is

Gold plating is a very thin layer of gold bonded onto a base metal such as brass, copper, stainless steel or sterling silver, usually by electroplating. Thickness is measured in microns, a millionth of a meter, and the layer is far thinner than a human hair.

Price and lifespan come down to two choices: how thick the gold is, and what metal sits beneath it.

Plated, vermeil, filled or solid?

These terms are often mixed up. Rules differ by country, so always ask for specifics.

The case for gold plating

  • Price. You get the gold look for a fraction of the cost.
  • Try before you commit. It is a low-risk way to test a trend or a metal tone.
  • Light and varied. Bold, large designs are affordable.
  • Good for gifts and travel. Less worry if a piece is lost.

The case against

  • It wears off. Friction, sweat and water thin the gold, and the base metal shows through.
  • Skin marks. Base metals can react with skin and leave a green or gray tint. It is harmless but annoying.
  • Sensitivity. Some base metals contain nickel. If your skin is sensitive, see the tips on the earring finder.
  • Hard to repair or resize. Re-plating is sometimes possible, but resizing plated rings is not.
  • Little resale value. The gold content is almost nothing.

When it is worth it, and when it is not

Worth it

  • Trend pieces you may tire of
  • Earrings and necklaces, which see less friction than rings
  • Special-occasion jewelry worn a few times a year

Not worth it

  • Rings and bracelets worn daily
  • Wedding or anniversary pieces
  • Anything you wear in the shower or gym

Cost per wear: a quick example

This is an illustration, not a quote. A $30 plated bracelet that looks good for about 60 wears costs 50 cents a wear. A $300 solid-gold bracelet worn 600 times over several years also costs 50 cents a wear, yet it is still there afterwards and keeps its metal value. How to buy better plated jewelry

  • Ask for thickness in microns. About 2.5 microns is commonly treated as heavy plating.
  • Check the base metal. Sterling silver or stainless steel is better than brass or copper.
  • Look for nickel-free. Especially for earrings.
  • Read the wording. “14k gold plated” does not mean 14k gold throughout. “Gold tone” means almost no gold.
  • Check returns. A fair return policy is a good sign.

Making it last

  1. Put it on last, after perfume, lotion and hairspray, and take it off first.
  2. Keep it away from water, sweat and pools.
  3. Wipe with a soft dry cloth after each wear.
  4. Store it dry, in a pouch, away from other pieces.
  5. Skip abrasive polish, which removes plating.

More material-by-material tips are in the care guide.

Verdict: buy plated for fashion and short-term pieces, and save your budget for solid gold or gold-filled where you wear it every day.

Frequently asked questions

Does gold-plated jewelry turn skin green?

It can. The base metal reacts with sweat and skin chemistry. Thicker plating and a barrier layer reduce it, and so does keeping jewelry dry.

Can I shower in gold-plated jewelry?

It is best not to. Water and soap speed up wear and tarnish.

How long does gold plating last?

From a few months to a couple of years, depending on thickness, how often you wear it and how you care for it.

Is gold-plated jewelry hypoallergenic?

Not necessarily. It depends on the base metal. Look for nickel-free labeling, or choose sterling silver, steel or solid gold if your skin is sensitive.
